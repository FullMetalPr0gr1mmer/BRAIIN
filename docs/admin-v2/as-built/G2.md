## What was built

- **`public.health_probes`**, one row per tenant and probe (`uptime`, `domain`, `certificate`):
  the state (`ok`, `warn`, `fail`, `info`, `unknown`, `off`), the reading's facts (a JSON object
  of at most 16 KB, parsed by Zod on every read: `packages/schemas/healthProbe.ts`, unknown keys
  dropped, strings and lists cut to their caps), whether the last attempt read its source, the
  failures in a row, and when it was last tried, last read well and may next be tried (the
  persisted backoff). RLS ENABLE + FORCE; one policy, `health_probes_read` (the tenant
  predicate, then `app.role_site_health`: Admin and Developer); staff SELECT, the service role
  SELECT INSERT UPDATE, nothing for PUBLIC or anon, no DELETE for anyone (rows go with their
  tenant, ON DELETE CASCADE); postconditions under both grant regimes, MAINTAIN on 17. The one
  trigger, `health_probes_stamps` (`app.tg_health_probe_stamps()`, invoker, empty search_path,
  executable by no API role), sets `updated_at` and moves `state_since` only when the state
  does or the probe goes stale (the failure count reaching 6, held equal to
  `THRESHOLDS.staleAfter` by `tests/lib/healthProbes.spec.ts`), so no writer can forge when a
  state began (the bell's "since"). The stale move was added in the pre-merge review: a probe
  never read is `unknown` from its first failure, so its stale item dated from that failure
  and a person who opened the bell in between got no dot.
- **The help set**, restated from 0065 (C4b), with only `site-health-and-logs` rewritten (EN
  and AR, block for block): the 40 / 40 / 20 score, the Uptime card (the strip's colours, from
  outside the hosting, every 10 minutes, Riyadh days, View as table, Check now 6 an hour, Not
  set up until a developer adds the key), the Security tab (the domain about daily, 30 days;
  the certificate from the monitor, 14 days, else Cloudflare) and what rings the bell. Slug,
  title, caps and screens kept; every other guide byte for byte (`helpCentre.spec.ts`).
- **Outbound calls**, `src/lib/http/outbound.ts`: a 5 s timeout over the request and the body, a
  256 KB cap read through a counting reader, one retry with 200 to 600 ms of jitter on a
  network error or a 5xx only (never a 4xx, never a timeout), `redirect: 'manual'` with at
  most one https hop for a GET (a POST never follows one: its body holds the monitor's key),
  retries and hops sharing a budget of two requests a call, https URLs with no credentials
  only, and errors that carry a reason code and a status, never a URL, a body or a key. R7's
  purge client should reuse it.
- **The uptime seam**, `src/lib/health/uptime/`: `UptimeProvider` and `uptimeProvider()` (null
  without `UPTIME_MONITOR_API_KEY`: "Not set up"); one adapter, UptimeRobot (D1).
- **RDAP**, `src/lib/health/rdap.ts` and `domain.ts`: the IANA bootstrap's longest TLD match
  (https only; the entry kept 30 days in the row), `<base>domain/<apex>`, the expiration event
  and the registrar's vCard name; a 404 is "no registration found"; no event, or a TLD no
  registry covers, is information. The apex is `tenants.primary_domain` lowercased, a trailing
  dot and one `www.` removed, validated as a hostname (LDH labels, at most 253, a dot, not an
  IP, punycode allowed); `*.workers.dev`, `*.pages.dev`, `localhost` and an empty value are not
  ours to renew.
- **The probe runner**, `src/lib/health/probes.ts`: one read (the tenant's domain with its
  probe rows, a PostgREST embed: one request), the due probes (every one with `force`) in
  parallel, the pure `nextProbe` for each next row, ONE upsert, and failures logged to
  `system_logs` (`warn`, reason codes only) on the first failure in a row, on the sixth (the
  probe goes stale) and on every Check now, so a provider down for a day writes two lines,
  not 144. Thresholds and timing (D10) are `THRESHOLDS` in `src/lib/health/thresholds.ts` and
  `TIMING` in `probes.ts`: uptime fails when a monitor is down or "seems down", warns under
  99.9% over 30 days; the domain fails at 30 days or fewer, or with no registration; the
  certificate fails at 14 days or fewer; a failed read is tried again on the schedule's next
  run (`TIMING.retryMs`), so six in a row, an hour after the last good reading, make a probe
  `unknown`; from then on the backoff is 10 minutes doubling to at most 6 hours, ±20%
  (`retryAfterMs`). Backing off from the first failure, as first built, made a probe stale
  only four to seven hours in, not the hour D10, the guide and the bell promise (pre-merge
  review; `tests/lib/healthProbes.spec.ts` walks the 10-minute ticks). The certificate is read with
  the uptime (no request of its own) and fails with it. Incidents keep when this Worker first
  saw them (`seenAt`).
- **The schedule dispatcher**, `src/lib/cron/schedules.ts`: `SCHEDULES` maps each cron string to
  its jobs (`'23 3 * * *'`: the daily jobs; `'*/10 * * * *'`: `HEALTH_JOBS`); `src/worker.ts`
  dispatches on `controller.cron`; a cron no schedule names runs nothing and leaves one warn
  line. `wrangler.jsonc` gains the second trigger. `src/lib/cron/daily.ts` keeps
  `runDailyJobs` and gains `runJobs` (any schedule's jobs, each isolated as before). U9b joins
  the health schedule by adding a job there (plan §5.7).
- **The health job**, `src/lib/cron/health.ts`: `health-probes`, budget 11 (the launch tenant
  1, the read 1, the monitor 2, the bootstrap 2, the registry 2, the upsert 1, one line 2),
  launch tenant only (D8). `tests/lib/cronSchedules.spec.ts` holds the schedules equal to
  wrangler.jsonc's crons, each schedule's budgets within 50, and runs the job at its worst
  (every outbound read retried or redirected, a failure logged): exactly 11.
- **Check now**, `POST /api/admin/site-health/check` (`siteHealth.view`): another tenant gets a
  409 (`single-site`) before anything is read or claimed; then `HEALTH_CHECK_LIMITS` (6 an hour
  per person, 12 per tenant, op `health.check`) on the privileged-op ledger, failing closed,
  429 with `Retry-After: 3600`; then the forced run as the service role. It answers the GET
  twin's three readings, `checkedAt` and the kinds that failed. No audit row (D7). The island
  `AdminHealthCheck` (`screens/health/CheckNow.tsx`, the ClearLogs pattern) posts and
  reloads; its errors show beside the button, never as a toast.
- **The screen**: the subtitle names the site's host and "Uptime checked <relTime>" once there
  is a reading; Check now in the page head for every role that sees the screen; Overview is
  two columns (`grid--3-2`, the kit's): the Uptime card beside the checklist. The card
  (`UptimeCard.astro`): the readings could not be read / Not set up / not checked yet / the
  monitor did not answer (with the last reading's age), or a reading: 30 days, 90 days and
  now ("Down since ..."); the 90-day strip (`UptimeStrip.astro`, an `aria-hidden` list of 90
  bars whose state is a data attribute, one row at 480 px and wider, three rows of 30 below)
  in `ChartFigure` with its summary ("Up all day on N of 90 days") and "View as table" (the
  days with downtime, newest first, Riyadh days); the outages (the provider's reason as text,
  the date, the minutes); the response time over 48 hours (the kit's `Line`, hourly
  averages, its table); the monitors ("/ · /ar/ · /healthz", another address named only as
  such) and a note when the standard's paths are not all watched. The interval in the
  sub-line is the monitors' real one. A **Security** tab (Card "Website": the four code
  facts, then the domain and the certificate). Tiles: LCP, INP, CLS, **Uptime, 30 days**
  ("99.96%", "Not set up", "No reading yet" once a run has tried and nothing has come in,
  "Not reported" when the monitor gives no 30-day figure, or "Could not be read"), errors,
  the checklist.
- **The checklist**: `security.domain` and `security.certificate`, `needs: ['siteHealth.view']`,
  after the code facts in the Security group, no fix links (the fix is at the registrar or the
  monitor; the detail says who to ask). `HealthContext.probes` (undefined: not read; null: the
  read failed).
- **The score**: weighted parts `SCORE_WEIGHTS = {speed: 40, checks: 40, uptime: 20}`,
  renormalised over the parts there are; uptime points `(ratio30 - 99) x 100` between 0 and
  100. Without a reading (off, never read, stale, or unreadable) it is U2's score and U2's
  formula prefix, word for word, ending "Uptime is not set up yet." or "Uptime could not be
  read just now."; U2's "Uptime is not measured here yet." is gone, in the same PR as the
  guide that quoted it.
- **Loaders**: `loadProbes` (`src/lib/admin/health/probes.ts`, one SELECT as the person) joins
  `loadHealthReport`'s `Promise.all` (five reads at most); `HealthReport` gains `probes` and
  `uptime`, `HealthSummary` gains `uptime` (additive: U1's widget shows the new tile through
  the tiles it already renders). `GET /api/admin/site-health` gains `uptime`, `domain` and
  `certificate` (state, checkedAt, okAt, the parsed facts).
- **The bell**: the `health` kind (`NOTIFICATION_KINDS`, after `maintenance`; mutable through
  F8's preferences), source `src/lib/admin/notifications/health.ts`, for `siteHealth.view`
  only: one read of `health_probes`; the site is down (since the state began; the down
  monitors named by path), each outage within the 14-day window (new from when it was first
  seen), the domain failing ("expires in N days", "No registration found for <apex>"), the
  certificate failing, a probe stale after six failed reads (not when off; the certificate's
  staleness rides on the uptime's). A slow month (`warn`) is not an item.
- **Env and secrets**: `UPTIME_MONITOR_API_KEY` (server secret, optional) and
  `UPTIME_MONITOR_IDS` (server, public, optional; the adapter checks
  `^\d{1,12}(-\d{1,12}){0,4}$` and refuses anything else without sending it) in
  `astro.config.mjs`; `scripts/push-secrets.mjs` pushes the key when `.env` names it.
- **Docs**: CLAUDE.md (the UI-lane bullet, §10's monitors and cron sentences, the Worker entry's
  crons), the launch runbook §6k (monitors, the read-only key, the ids, alert contacts,

## The provider's API (D1), verified

UptimeRobot's **legacy v2** `POST https://api.uptimerobot.com/v2/getMonitors`, checked against
https://uptimerobot.com/api/legacy/ on 2026-10-18: `api_key` (a read-only key covers every
`get*` method), `monitors` (ids joined by `-`), `custom_uptime_ratios` (`30-90`),
`custom_uptime_ranges` (`<start>_<end>` Unix seconds joined by `-`), `logs`, `log_types` (`1`:
down), `logs_limit`, `response_times`, `response_times_average` (minutes),
`response_times_start_date`/`_end_date` (a span of at most 7 days), `ssl`, `limit` (at most 50).
Status 0 paused, 1 not checked yet, 2 up, 8 seems down, 9 down; log types 1 down, 2 up, 98
started, 99 paused; `stat` ok or fail. The `ssl` object's fields (`brand`, `product`,
`expires`) and the log's `reason` (`code`, `detail`) are not in that page's text: they are read
leniently (absent or malformed: no certificate, "Down" or "Error <code>"), and the fixtures in
`tests/lib/uptimeRobot.spec.ts` pin the shapes this adapter reads. A v3 REST API exists
(https://uptimerobot.com/api/v3/); the adapter stays on v2 (the documented read-only key and
one call for everything); a move to v3 is one file.

## Measured (O-16)

- **A health run's CPU**: parsing a five-monitor answer (18 KB: 90 ranges, 10 logs and 48
  response times each), normalising it, carrying the incidents and building the next row
  took 0.9 ms a run on Node 22 on the build machine (200 runs after a warm-up). The Worker's
  10 ms is CPU only (waiting on the network is free); measure it in production with Workers
  Logs once the key is set (runbook §6k).
- **A health run's subrequests**: at most 11 (above, `cronSchedules.spec.ts`); 2 on a normal run
  (the read and the upsert; 3 with the launch tenant) plus 1 for the monitor, plus 1 or 2 on
  the day the domain is due.
- **A site-health render**: one more read than U2's (five at most). **Every admin render for
  Admin and Developer**: one more read, the bell's health source. The dashboard's budget test
  (`tests/lib/dashboardWidgets.spec.ts`) moves from U1's 24 + 34 to 25 + 36 of 50 (the widget's
  `loadHealthSummary` reads the probes, and the shell's bell reads them again); with R5's read of
  stale pending changes (0066) merged in, it measures 26 + 37.

## Departures from the design, and why

- **RDAP is not TLS.** The README row says "SSL/domain expiry via RDAP". RDAP (RFC 9082/9083)
  carries the domain's registration and its `expiration` event; it says nothing about
  certificates, and a Worker cannot read the certificate it was served (`fetch()` and
  `cloudflare:sockets` expose no peer certificate). So the domain's expiry comes from RDAP and
  the certificate's from the monitor when it reports one; otherwise the row is information,
  "Certificates are issued and renewed by Cloudflare", never a guess (D3; not CT logs: another
  third party, and they say what was issued, not what is served).
- **Not the daily cron.** The README row puts RDAP "in the daily cron"; its jobs already declare
  49 of the 50 subrequests (`tests/lib/dailyJobs.spec.ts`; 42 when the plan was written, U14a's
  re-sync added 7), and uptime must be read far more often than daily. G2 adds a second
  trigger, `*/10 * * * *` (the TDD's "every ten minutes"), its own invocation with its own 50;
  RDAP runs inside it when the domain row is due (20 hours after its last good read), so about
  daily (D2).
- **The bell carries the probe failures, not the whole checklist.** The bell's loader runs on
  every admin render (F8); the full checklist costs four reads. One read of `health_probes`
  carries what only G2 knows (D5). The dashboard's health widget (U1) already lists the
  failing checklist rows.
- **§8's row shape**, as U2 did for `rollup_daily_vitals`: a derived snapshot keyed by what it
  describes, `(tenant_id, kind)`: no `id`, no `created_by`/`updated_by` (no person writes a
  row), no `version` (nobody edits it; no 409 path). `tenant_id`, RLS ENABLE + FORCE and the
  tenant predicate first are kept.
- **No Alerts drawer.** Its channels (e-mail, WhatsApp, Slack) are A1/L6; its "when to tell me"
  toggles are the bell's mute (the `health` kind is mutable through F8's preferences API, and
  U11's notification settings screen will label it). The monitor's own e-mail alerts are set up in
  the monitor (runbook §6k): the provider's channel, not an outbound send of ours.
- **The guide does not say the bell can be muted from a screen.** The brief asks the bell item
  to say "that it can be muted"; no screen offers muting until U11 (only F8's API does), and
  the help spec holds the guides to what the admin does. U11's restatement adds the sentence
  with the settings screen.
- **The health job's budget is 11, not 8.** The brief's 8 counted one request per outbound read;
  a retry and a redirect hop are subrequests too, so each outbound read declares its worst
  case, 2, and the failure line its 2 (tenant lookup and insert). 11 of 50.
- **The failure lines are throttled.** The brief logs "only failures"; logging every failed run
  of a 10-minute schedule writes 144 lines a day while a provider is down and drags the Errors
  tab. The run logs the first failure in a row, the sixth (stale) and every Check now, at
  level `warn` (a provider outage is not a site error, and `errors.recent` counts `error`).
- **The job's log source is `cron:health-probes`**, the runner's `cron:<name>` convention, so a
  thrown run and a failed read are filed under one name (the brief named `cron:health`).
- **A domain value that is not a hostname** fails the row ("The site's domain, <value>, is not a
  domain name") rather than reading as information: it is a misconfiguration someone must fix,
  and the row names it.
- **The Check now 409 is `single-site`**, not the kernel's `not-set-up`, whose shared wording
  ("needs email delivery") belongs to the invites; the island words it ("Uptime is set up for
  one site only").
- **The Uptime card's "never read", stale and "unknown" wording** is two pure functions in
  `src/lib/admin/health/uptime.ts`, one line per state, held by `tests/lib/uptimeLines.spec.ts`
  and the card's render spec. They count the tries rather than claim "an hour" (Check now can
  make six failures in minutes), and once a probe is stale they give the backoff's real bound:
  6 hours with +20% jitter plus up to one run of the schedule is 7 h 22 min, so
  `STALE_RETRY_HOURS` = 8 (the spec holds it to `TIMING`). With no reading to show
  (`noReadingLine`):
  - no row, or no failures and no reading: "Not checked yet. Readings arrive every 10 minutes."
  - no failures, a reading that came in but does not parse: "The last reading could not be
    shown. Readings arrive every 10 minutes."
  - never read, 1 to 5 failures: "No reading yet: the monitor did not answer the last N tries.
    It is asked again every 10 minutes." ("the last try" for one)
  - never read and stale (`failures >= THRESHOLDS.staleAfter`, state `unknown`): "No reading
    yet: the monitor did not answer the last N tries. It is asked less often now, at least once
    every 8 hours; Check now asks at once."

  Above an older reading when the last try failed (`failedReadLine`, a warn note): "The monitor
  did not answer the last N tries." then the same pace sentence for the state (every 10
  minutes, or at least once every 8 hours once stale) and "Last reading <relTime>."
- **Overview's two columns are the kit's `grid--3-2`**, not a new `.hs__cols` grid
  (`.hs__cols` only aligns the cards to the top).

## Decisions (defaults taken)

| # | Question | Taken |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Monitor provider | UptimeRobot: a genuinely read-only key, a free tier, SSL expiry where the plan has it. One adapter file behind `UptimeProvider` |
| D2 | Daily cron or own schedule | Own trigger, `*/10 * * * *`, dispatched by cron string; RDAP from it about daily |
| D3 | Certificate expiry source | The monitor when it reports it; otherwise "issued and renewed by Cloudflare" (information) |
| D4 | RESTRICTIVE live check on `health_probes` | No: as `rollup_daily_vitals`; no personal data; a role change revokes sessions since U12a. pgTAP documents the stale token (a demoted Admin still reads until the token expires) |
| D5 | "Failing checks in the bell" | The probe failures and outages only, one read for Admin and Developer |
| D6 | A nav count on Site health | No |
| D7 | Audit Check now | No audit row; the ledger records who and when; failures go to `system_logs` |
| D8 | Tenancy | Launch tenant only; other tenants see "Not set up"; the route answers 409 for them before any query |
| D9 | Strip days | Asia/Riyadh (UTC+3 all year), "Day (Riyadh)" in the table |
| D10 | Thresholds | Domain fails at 30 days or fewer; certificate at 14 or fewer; uptime warns under 99.9% over 30 days; stale after 6 failed reads (an hour) |
| D11 | Score | 40 / 40 / 20 with a reading, U2's halves without, renormalised over the parts present |
| D12 | Check now | An island (the ClearLogs pattern); without JS the button is absent and the schedule still reads |

unchanged. If another slice restates the set before G2 merges, re-base again from 0065 (w3
corrections rule 1).
