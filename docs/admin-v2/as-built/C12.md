### SQL (0138)

- `crm_merge_dismissals` (tenant_id, contact_a < contact_b, created_by, created_at; the key on
  the three ids; both contacts by composite keys to the tenant's own, ON DELETE CASCADE). RLS
  ENABLE + FORCE; `crm.contacts` reads and adds (tenant predicate first), a RESTRICTIVE live
  check for all commands (H16); grants stated for both regimes (P-14): authenticated SELECT and
  INSERT on the three named columns, the service role all, anon and PUBLIC nothing. A BEFORE
  trigger (a definer) holds an API insert to its own tenant and a live role first (C8-SEC-2's
  lesson: a trigger runs before RLS's WITH CHECK), stamps who and when, and refuses a tombstone.
- The finder, in two layers: `app.crm_duplicate_candidates(p_limit)`, a pinned definer (it
  compares blind indexes, which no staff token reads), empty unless the caller's LIVE role holds
  `crm.contacts`, always the token's own tenant; then `public.crm_duplicate_pairs(p_tenant,
  p_limit)`, SECURITY INVOKER for `authenticated`, which reads both contacts of each pair through
  RLS (names, title, company, stage, dates, version, the counts of linked leads and of e-mails and
  phones; never a value). Rules: the same phone blind index; the same `name_key` and the same
  folded company. Dismissed pairs and tombstones are left out; at most 50, phone matches first.
  A btree on (tenant_id, name_key) for live contacts.
- `public.crm_dismiss_duplicate(p_tenant, p_a, p_b)`: INVOKER for `authenticated` ("Not a
  duplicate"), both must be live contacts the caller sees (P0002), idempotent.
- `public.crm_merge_contacts(p_tenant, p_keep, p_keep_version, p_drop, p_drop_version, p_actor)`:
  INVOKER, the service role's alone (as C6's doors: it moves channels, notes, leads' links and
  events, which no staff token may write), checking the actor live (`app.crm_contacts_actor_ok`).
  One transaction: both rows locked in id order, P0002 for a contact the tenant does not hold or
  a tombstone, 40001 on either version, the e-mail locks the link takes (so an arrival never
  links to the record going away), the channel ceiling (23514 `crm_contact_channels_cap`), then
  the survivor's blanks filled, tags united (at most 10), star ORed, custom values filled, stage
  by precedence (client > lead > past > partner); channels, notes, tasks, leads, timeline lines
  and dismissals moved; a `merged` line; the dropped record made a scrubbed tombstone
  (`merged_into`, no name, tags, custom values or owner); and the `crm.contact.merge` audit row
  in the same transaction.
- `app.purge_crm` restated (C5's copy plus lines marked `-- C12`): tombstones go on the next run,
  after anything that raced onto one is moved to its survivor.
- Postconditions for both grant regimes.

### Departures from the design, and why

1. **The merge is the service role's door, not an INVOKER RPC run as the caller** (crm.md §5.5,
   §9). It moves channels, notes, leads' links and timeline lines, which C5 gave no staff token
   (and should not: a column grant cannot tell roles apart). So it follows C6's doors: INVOKER,
   executable by the service role alone, checking the acting profile live in SQL; the Worker
   rechecks live first, applies a limit and reads both people as the caller (RLS: a 404 for
   another tenant's or a merged record). The audit row is still written inside the merge's own
   transaction, naming the actor (`actor_id` = the acting profile, `actor_role` its role).
2. **No domain rule in the finder yet.** crm.md §9's third rule (the same non-freemail e-mail
   domain plus a similar name) needs a domain index on the channels, which C5 left to C11
   (Companies); there is no domain index to compare. The finder ships with the phone rule and
   the name-and-company rule; the domain rule is a restatement of
   `app.crm_duplicate_candidates` once channels carry `domain_hmac`.
3. **Names and companies are compared folded and equal** (`app.crm_name_key` on both), not by
   trigram similarity: crm.md's similarity was part of the domain rule. A name-only rule would
   offer every pair of common names.
4. **A merge is activity** (the kept record's clock starts again), as C5 already counted a
   merge (`merged` among the lead events that move a contact's activity). C12 writes no lead-side
   `merged` line: the lead's own "Linked to a contact" line says it changed contact (C5's trigger
   draws it; the merge puts it down to the actor), and the contact's `merged` line says the rest.
   Register L-5 question 3 asks the lawyer whether a merge should restart the clock.
5. **Moved channels keep where they came from** (`lead`, `manual`), not `merge` (C5 reserved
   the word): erasing a lead takes back only the copies it carried by their source `lead`
   (C5 review), and a channel a person added keeps an auto-contact alive by its source `manual`.
6. **Notes are moved by re-adding** them with their ids, words, authors and times and removing
   the originals in the same transaction: no API role may UPDATE a note (append-only), and the
   words never change.
7. **Dismissals follow the survivor**, and **tombstones pointing at the merged record point at
   the survivor**, so a pair said not to be one stays said, and there are no tombstone chains.
8. **"endpoints.spec rows: admin and sales allowed"** (crm.md CRM-12): Admin only today, since
   `crm.contacts` is Admin's until C10b (built in parallel) gives it to Sales; C10b's flip of the
   capability flips these rows.
9. **The merge's limit** is its own ledger key (`crm-merge`) at the manual add's numbers (60 an
   hour per person, 300 per tenant): a merge is irreversible, so a stolen session cannot fold the
   tenant's people together faster than anyone could notice; real cleanup stays inside it.
10. **The tombstone purge also moves what raced onto a tombstone** (a lead linked or a task added
    in the instant of the merge) to its survivor before deleting it; the merge itself takes the
    link's per-address e-mail locks first, so an arrival from one of those addresses waits and
    then finds the survivor.
