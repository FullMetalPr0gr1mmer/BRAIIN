# C3: the leads write API

- **Lane:** CRM
- **Design:** [crm.md](../crm.md) CRM-3 (the write side), §4.2 to §4.4 (the columns it
  writes), §7.2 (manual add), §8.2 (erasure, for a lead), §9 (routes)
- **PR:** not opened yet (branch `feat/c3-leads-write-api`)
- **Migrations:** 0041 (`0041_crm_leads_write.sql`), expand. The design's file was 0032, a
  number taken since; 0041 is the Wave 1 reservation.

## What was built

- **`crm.erase`**, Admin only, in `ROLE_CAPS`, CLAUDE.md §5, architecture §3.4 and §9.3 (33
  capabilities), with its SQL helper `app.role_crm_erase` (held equal to `ROLE_CAPS` by
  `tests/authz/sqlHelpers.spec.ts`).
- **The columns the write API sets** on `leads`: `assigned_to` (composite key to a profile of
  the same tenant, `ON DELETE SET NULL (assigned_to)`), `value_sar`, `is_starred`,
  `read_at`/`read_by`, `tags` (`app.tags_ok`), `last_contact_at`/`last_contact_channel`, all
  readable by staff and appended to `leads_safe`. Staff UPDATE grows from 0030's legacy pair to
  the pipeline's columns; `read_by` is never granted.
- **The two trigger phases restated whole** (their step order is the contract): the BEFORE
  phase adds the assignee check, the read mark (first look stands, the session is who looked),
  the logged contact (server time), a first response stamped by a contact logged while the
  lead is still new, and the version moving with the assignee, value and tags too. Looking,
  starring and logging a contact never move the version, and looking or starring is not an
  edit (`updated_at` stays). The AFTER phase adds `assigned`/`unassigned`, `value_set` and
  `contact_logged` events.
- **RPCs:** `public.leads_bulk_update` (SECURITY INVOKER, `authenticated` only, one call: one
  statement changes the leads, a second judges the rest; every item applied, conflict, missing
  or skipped); `public.crm_erase_lead`,
  `public.crm_delete_lead_note` and `public.crm_lead_note_counts` (service role only);
  `public.crm_ingest_lead` restated so a manual lead names who added it and arrives answered
  and read.
- **Routes:** versioned `PATCH /api/admin/leads/[id]`; `POST /api/admin/leads/bulk`;
  `POST /api/admin/leads` (manual add); `POST /api/admin/leads/[id]/erase`;
  `DELETE /api/admin/leads/[id]/notes/[noteId]`; the CSV export's filters and columns.
- **`writeAuditMany`** (`src/lib/admin/audit.ts`): one insert for many audit rows.

## Departures from the design, and why

- **C3 adds the columns it writes.** The design put assignee, value, star, read, tags and last
  contact in CRM-1; C1a-2 deferred them "to the slices that use them". The write API is the
  first, so 0041 adds them, with their grants, checks, events and the `leads_safe` append.
- **The readers are not extended.** `leads_list`, `leads_board` and `lead_summary` (0036) do
  not return the new columns or filter by assignee, star or read state yet, and the legacy
  `GET /api/admin/leads/[id]` keeps its shape (the PATCH answers with the new columns). C4,
  which builds the screens that show them, adds that; so does the design's single-lead detail
  GET that sets `read_at` (C2b did not build it either). No GIN index on `tags` until a filter
  uses it.
- **`internalNotes` is still accepted** by the PATCH (plan default: the 422 moves to C4, while
  the legacy LeadsPanel exists). A legacy `status` cannot be sent with a stage or spam change.
- **Manual add matches earlier leads by blind index only** (plan default B7-12): the same
  e-mail is added and named in the answer (`sameEmail`, safe fields), a phone match with no
  e-mail match is a 409 `possible-duplicate` with the matches unless `createNew`. Leads marked
  spam count: `leads_list` shows the pipeline or the spam, never both, so each index is looked
  up in both (two calls in parallel, as the caller), the pipeline's matches first, and each
  match carries `spam` so the screen can say which. The design's
  `linkContactId` waits for contacts (C5). The channels are the `LEAD_CHANNELS` subset phone,
  walk_in, whatsapp, email, event, referral and `unknown` (the design's "other"). The service
  slug is checked for shape, not for being published (the drawer offers published ones).
  `consent_marketing` stays false: it records the public form's checkbox only.
- **Manual add is rate-limited, and its 409 is audited.** The design gave it neither. It takes
  the reveal's numbers through the privileged-op ledger (`lead-manual-add`, 60 an hour per
  person and 300 per tenant, `MANUAL_LEAD_LIMITS`, fail-closed), claimed after the live recheck
  and before any lookup: each add writes contact details as the service role and probes earlier
  leads by blind index, so the limit bounds both. A possible duplicate writes
  `lead.create.duplicate` (source, channel and the matched leads' ids, never what was typed),
  because the lookup happened even though nothing was added.
- **The erase and note-delete doors check the actor themselves.** The design's
  `crm_erase_lead(p_tenant, p_lead)` takes `p_actor` too, and both doors refuse (42501) unless
  that profile is active, unlocked, in the tenant and holds `crm.erase` through
  `app.role_crm_erase`, read live (`app.crm_eraser_ok`). The service role is trusted anyway;
  this makes the helper load-bearing and gives pgTAP something to prove.
- **The erase route reads the lead under RLS before the attempt row** (as the reveal does), so
  an unseen lead is a 404 with nothing written. Counts come in the outcome row, not the
  attempt (only the service role can count notes). Erasing a person, with contacts and
  phone-only matches, is C5's.
- **Deleting the legacy mirror note clears `internal_notes`** too, inside the door: deleting the
  mirror alone would leave the words in the field, and the next legacy save would restore them.
  The door clears it as the deleting person (named in `app.acting_for` for that one write), and
  every deleted note leaves a `note_deleted` event naming them, with the note's id and source
  only. That is a new
  timeline kind (the `lead_events` kind CHECK is widened, an expansion), written by the door
  rather than a trigger, because only the door knows who acted.
  The note delete is not rate-limited (the design gives it none); it is live-rechecked and
  audited fail-closed.
- **A service-role lead write names the person it acts for.** It has no session, so
  `auth.uid()` is null: the BEFORE phase now takes a manual lead's creator as its `updated_by`,
  and on an edit takes the person a service-role door names in `app.acting_for`, a setting the
  door sets with `set_config(…, true)` just before its write and puts back just after; the
  AFTER phase's events take the person the BEFORE phase recorded. The session's own person
  always comes first, so staff cannot name anyone, and no policy or check reads the setting:
  it changes who a write is put down to, never what anyone may do. Before this, a manual lead
  arrived last changed by no one and its `created` event named no one, and the note door's
  field clear left the lead edited by no one with no timeline row.
  The first fix had the door write `updated_by = p_actor` and the BEFORE phase keep a value
  that differed from the old one. The review found that fails in the common case: whoever
  deletes a legacy mirror usually saved its field last, so the value is the old one, reads as
  unchanged, and the lead was again edited by no one (pgTAP had saved the field with no
  session first, so it never met the case). The setting replaces it, and the "differs" rule is
  gone rather than kept behind it: the door was its only user, and a rule that fails exactly
  when the value is the same would leave the next door the same trap. pgTAP now saves the field
  as the admin who then deletes the mirror, and proves the naming ends with the door's write.
- **The note delete writes two audit rows, not the design's one `lead.note.delete`.** The
  attempt (`lead.note.delete.attempt`, fail-closed) is written before anyone knows whether the
  note is on that lead, so the outcome (`lead.note.delete.outcome`: `ok` with the note's source,
  or `not_found`, `refused`, `failed` with the code) says what happened; a 404 never reads as a
  deletion. Erase and note delete both write the failed outcome when the door cannot be
  reached at all (a thrown call), not only when it answers with an error.
- **The read mark and the logged contact take the server's clock** (the Worker sends "now",
  the BEFORE phase stamps `now()`), and `read_by` has no UPDATE grant. The design granted
  `read_by` and left the times to the client.
- **The export** reads keyset pages on `(created_at, id)`, newest first (`src/lib/crm/exportRead.ts`
  pages, the route builds each page's query). One unpaged read was silently cut at PostgREST's
  default 1,000 rows, so the documented 5,000 cap never held (found here); offset pages over a
  moving table repeat or skip a row when a lead arrives mid-export, and stopping at the first
  short page truncated silently under a lower row limit. So the read asks for one row past the
  cap, carries on from the last key until a page comes back empty, and stops at 20 pages
  (`truncated` when it cannot tell). Its filters take the list's defaults: spam only when
  asked (`spam=true`, or a legacy `status=spam` link), `from` inclusive and `to` exclusive, so
  the legacy panel's plain export link no longer includes spam. It refuses `q` in its link
  (D10) and reads note counts through `crm_lead_note_counts`, which answers one object so the
  row limit cannot cut it short. The CSV's columns changed: `internal_notes` is gone; the
  number, stage, spam, assignee, value, tags, score, source, channel and `notes_count` are
  added. This is a visible change to an existing export and is listed here for the decisions
  record.
- **The export carries 80 leads with contact details, 300 without, not 5,000.** The review
  found the cap set by memory when CPU is what runs out: every role holding `export.csv` holds
  `leads.pii`, so every export decrypts four fields a lead, and each decrypt derived and
  imported the key again. `decryptPII` now also takes a `CryptoKey` (`importPIIKey`; the
  other callers keep passing the material), the export imports it once a request, and the cap
  (`EXPORT_ROW_CAP`) is what the measurement below fits in the Free plan's 10 ms. That is
  below the 1,000 the export effectively carried before C3 (the review's ceiling), so a large
  pipeline now needs several exports (date ranges) or a larger plan (Open, below). An
  export that reaches the cap says so three ways: `truncated: true` in its outcome row, an
  `x-export-truncated` header (with `x-export-rows`) for a screen that fetches it, and a file
  name a plain download link shows (`leads-<day>-first-<n>.csv`). The abnormal-volume warning
  (§3), which fired at 1,000 rows and so could no longer fire, now fires when an export reaches
  its cap. The CSV is built before the outcome row is written, so an `ok` outcome means the
  file exists, and every handled failure after the attempt row (a page, a lookup, the key
  import) writes a `failed` outcome. Only a Worker stopped for CPU leaves an attempt alone,
  which is what the cap prevents.
- **Bulk audit rows** carry field names, the action and the stage id, never a tag's words
  (staff type them, and they can name people).
- **A bulk change whose audit insert fails still stands, and says so.** The audit rows name
  what changed, so they are written after it and cannot gate it (unlike the erase's attempt
  row). When `writeAuditMany` fails, the answer carries `audited: false` (it is `true`
  otherwise) so the screen can tell the person, and a `system_logs` line (`admin:leads-bulk`,
  level error) records the action and the counts, never ids or tag words, beside the audit
  sink's own line. No Sentry call: the Worker carries no server Sentry SDK (Sentry is the
  browser's, through the tunnel), so `system_logs` is where the server's errors land and what
  alerting reads.
- **Bulk judges the leads it did not change in a second statement.** The design had one
  statement. Inside it, a lead another session changed while the UPDATE waited on it (READ
  COMMITTED re-checks the version and leaves the row) still showed its old, matching version
  to the classification, which answered "skipped" for what is a conflict. The function is
  VOLATILE, so its second statement takes a fresh snapshot and sees the version that won.
  pgTAP has one session, so it proves the stale-version case (a conflict even where the tag
  guard would also skip) and `tests/lib/crmLeadsWriteSql.spec.ts` pins the two-statement shape;
  the two-session race itself is not exercised by any test.

## Side effects of the assignee key

`leads_assignee_fk` is `(tenant_id, assigned_to) → profiles (tenant_id, id)`, `ON DELETE SET NULL
(assigned_to)`, `ON UPDATE NO ACTION`. 0041 drops it before the unique constraint it depends on
(`profiles_tenant_id_id`), so the file runs twice cleanly (`tests/lib/crmLeadsWriteSql.spec.ts`).

- **A profile that moves tenant.** Changing `profiles.tenant_id` is refused (23503) while that
  person is assigned leads in the old tenant, because the key's referenced pair would change
  under them (pgTAP proves it). Unassign their leads first. Nothing in the app moves a profile
  between tenants; the owner does it by hand, and the standard already requires their sessions
  to be revoked when it happens.
- **A tenant that is deleted.** `tenants` cascades to `profiles` (0001) and to `leads` (0002),
  in that order (the order the keys were created). Deleting the profiles first fires the
  assignee key's SET NULL on the tenant's assigned leads, which runs the BEFORE and AFTER
  phases on them: the AFTER phase writes an `unassigned` event naming a tenant that is already
  deleted, and the event's own tenant key should then refuse it, failing the whole deletion.
  This is reasoned from the cascade order, not exercised by a test (no path in the product
  deletes a tenant). Whoever deletes a tenant by hand unassigns or deletes its leads first.

## The export's CPU, measured

`node scripts/bench-lead-export.mjs` (kept in the repo so the cap can be re-measured when the
export's per-row work changes) runs the real `src/lib/crypto/pii.ts` and
`src/lib/admin/csv.ts` in workerd through Miniflare, which wrangler already installs: no
bindings, no network, synthetic rows only. Each request parses a page of leads as PostgREST
sends it, builds the records (decrypting four fields for a PII row) and the CSV; a row's cost is
the slope between 200 and 1,200 rows, so what a request costs anyway cancels out. Five runs on
an Intel i7-8750H (2018 laptop), 2026-10-10, the fastest of 15 timings each:

| Per row | Message of 400 characters | Message of 5,000 (the form's longest) |
|---|---|---|
| Without contact details | 6.5 to 11.3 µs | 15.4 to 19.2 µs |
| With them, key imported once a request (now) | 40.7 to 54.9 µs | 57.4 to 73.6 µs |
| With them, key imported per decrypt (before) | 93.0 to 117.0 µs | 112.5 to 141.2 µs |

One AES-GCM decrypt in workerd is about 9 µs (a separate micro-run), most of a PII row;
reusing one `TextDecoder` and reading the IV and ciphertext as views, not copies, took about
2 µs off each. Importing the key once halved a PII row. The caps give a full export's rows at
most 6 ms at the worst rate measured (80 × 74 µs = 5.9 ms, 300 × 19 µs = 5.7 ms) and leave 4
ms for the rest of the request (routing, the session, the audit rows, the other reads'
answers), which this script cannot measure: it needs the whole Worker and a database (owner
item O-16 measures it on staging). Before the review, the 1,000 rows a PII export carried cost
roughly 100 to 140 ms of CPU. `leadExport.spec` pins both caps under those rates and the PII
cap under 1,000.

## Open

- **The export cap is low for a large pipeline.** On the Workers Free plan an export carries
  80 leads with contact details, and the export limit is 3 an hour a person, 10 a tenant, so a
  thousand leads take 13 exports split by date, over four hours. Workers Paid ($5 a
  month, 30 s of CPU a request, owner item O-16) would let the cap go back toward 1,000 or
  more (under 0.1 s of CPU), bounded then by memory; so would a paged export (a cursor from one
  export to the next), which no slice has designed. For the owner to decide; until then the
  file's name and header say when it was cut.
- **The rest of the export request was not measured.** The cap leaves it 4 ms. The owner
  checklist's O-16 (and P-15) in the [README](../README.md) now list
  `GET /api/admin/leads/export` at the cap (80 leads with contact details) for the staging
  measurement.
